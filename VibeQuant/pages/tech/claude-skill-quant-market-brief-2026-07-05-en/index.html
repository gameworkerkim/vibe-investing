What Changed Since Yesterday

  • Crash → V-shaped rebound: KOSPI plunged 7.9% the prior day on the US semiconductor shock, then reclaimed the 8,000 level within a single day. The direction didn't change — the buyer base flipped from foreign to institutional.
  • Non-farm payroll shock: US June non-farm payrolls came in at 57,000 (vs. 110,000 expected), with April and May combined revised down 74,000 → expectations for Fed rate hikes eased. This is the first crack in the "hawkish Fed" narrative that has dominated the year.
  • Sharp KRW reversal: driven by the non-farm shock, the yen's strength following hints of intervention from Japan's finance minister, and Korean FX authorities' own intervention, the exchange rate dropped sharply from around ₩1,548 to around ₩1,525. However, this coincides with expectations for the Bank of Korea beginning rate hikes in July, so we're entering a week with monetary-policy events.

Falsification Conditions

  • If this was Risk-Off, not Rotation: we'd simultaneously observe NASDAQ/semiconductors falling sharply again when the US reopens Monday, VIX breaking 16→20 (a jump of +20% or more is a separate warning threshold), foreign selling continuing for a 12th day, and the exchange rate re-entering the ₩1,540s.
  • Conversely, if this was a return to Risk-On: KOSDAQ and the Russell 2000 would outperform large caps, and breadth would flip to new-highs > new-lows. Right now it's neither — a middle ground.

Structural Anomaly

The gap between VIX at 16 and VKOSPI at 96 is not a figure that arises in a normal market. The prevailing interpretation is that concentrated flows in single-stock leveraged ETFs on Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix are a localized structural problem amplifying Korean volatility, but in a market where 10% intraday swings keep repeating, this should be read with the caveat that the reliability of factor signals themselves is diminished.
